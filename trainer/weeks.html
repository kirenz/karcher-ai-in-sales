You do not need a launch. One prepared session inside a meeting that already exists, then the same again for the next group. Everything here runs on material that already exists.

Before your first session

  • Prepare your session: try the exercise, read one run sheet, copy the deck, rehearse ten minutes with a colleague. About an hour.
  • Book the slot: 30, 60 or 90 minutes, whichever you can get. Fifteen minutes inside a team meeting also work, with the first-look deck.
  • Check Workspace and Gemini access with participants a few days ahead, so there is time to fix it. If one device does not work on the day, pair that person with a colleague.

Your first session

Run the run sheet as it is written. Every participant leaves with one result they produced and checked themselves; that is the whole goal of the first session.

  • Protect the hands-on minutes; they matter more than finishing your slides.
  • End with the handoff: "now run the same recipe on one of your own lists."
  • Put the platform link in the meeting notes; it stays there from now on.

Keep it going

Usage survives on rhythm, not enthusiasm. Three small habits, all inside the team meeting you already have:

  • The link stays put. The platform link lives in the meeting notes, every week.
  • The card of the week. Name one card: one use case, one habit. Rotate through the tasks your team does most.
  • The next group, or the longer format. Run the same run sheet for the next team, or step up to 60 or 90 minutes with the group you already had. Only if a group needs something different, adapt the session.

When usage goes quiet

Every rollout dips after the first weeks. The novelty wears off, a busy quarter lands, and the platform stops being opened. Expect it, and treat it as maintenance, not as failure.

  • Restart small: a fifteen-minute refresher in the team meeting, one card, live. Not a new training.
  • Let a colleague show a win. A peer demo beats a trainer demo.
  • Match the card to the season: pipeline and report cards before reviews and year-end, research cards before fair season, translation cards when campaigns localize.

Do not answer a dip with a bigger training, and do not shame the team for not using it. Both raise the cost of coming back. The way back in is the same as the way in: one small card, inside a meeting that already exists.
