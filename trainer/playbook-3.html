When the output is wrong, slow down

A table that does not match its source rows is a useful opportunity to practise the check. Add the segment together out loud, name the difference, and ask which of the two numbers they would send to their manager. Do not explain why the model erred, and do not take the keyboard. The rehearsal sheet practises this situation directly.

Demoing live

Confirm sign-ins before you start, not at the first prompt. Read a prompt aloud as you copy it, so people follow the thinking rather than the keystrokes. Use the deck’s screenshots and written examples to illustrate the workflow. Compare the result of your own demonstration with the rows in front of you. In the sales-cycle sprint, the Deep Research run on ISS (issworld.com, a real public facility-services company) is the slowest moment: start it live, walk the room through the NorthStar buying center while it works, then come back to it and verify one claim against its source in front of everyone.

The data rules

In approved Kärcher Workspace tools, signed in with the Kärcher account, teams may work with real customer data: the contract keeps it confidential and the tools do not learn from it. Two obligations stay with the user: share only what the task needs, and validate every claim before it reaches a customer. Formal proposals, pricing, legal and contract terms keep the normal sign-off. Say this in the first ten minutes of any session where real accounts might be opened. Nothing in the practice exercises requires it, because all of them run on fictional material.

Prepared answers to the recurring worries (data security, wrong answers, “will AI replace me”, CI, effort) are on the hub’s FAQ page.

The sales-cycle sprint (optional, half or full day)

For a group that asks for the whole cycle. The deck is ttt-9-course-sprint; the content is the nine course steps on the platform, which carry the exact prompt, the attachment and the example Doc on screen. Present from the platform, and name the hand-off out loud, because every step hands its output to the next: lead → sourced briefing and Go/No-Go → buying-center map → first email → need profile → value argument → demo storyboard → signal read → follow-up. Underneath it runs the value hypothesis: it forms in research (step 2), feeds the first email (step 3) and the discovery questions (step 5), is confirmed against the real need, and becomes the substantiated value argument (step 6). Rough budget: opening and data rules 20 minutes, then 15 to 30 minutes per step, with steps 2, 5 and 6 getting the depth. Cluster B groups replace steps 1 to 8 with the Your-data cards, step 9 and the leadership chapters, about 30 minutes.

The per-step goals, lead Gems and “don’t skip” points live with the steps themselves on the platform, which is where they stay current.
