1. Run Your First Session

Everything a first session needs is already written. You pick a length, show a fixed set of slides, run the exercises on supplied practice material, and make sure the result gets checked.

The practice tracker is a prepared Google Sheets spreadsheet with twelve fictional customer accounts. Its rows contain example sales stages, industries, values in euros and notes. You will copy it and use Gemini to summarise the values. The preparation guide explains each step, including where to find the instructions and how to check a total.

The three steps

  1. Do the task yourself, about fifteen minutes: open See your pipeline at a glance, copy the practice tracker, run the prompt, and check one segment total by hand. You have now done exactly what your participants will do.
  2. Read the run sheet, alongside the deck notes: the session, 60 minutes, or 90 with the email exercise.
  3. Read it through once, about ten minutes: the run sheet minute by minute and the script page for the slides you show. Note the moment you want to say slowly; the Prepare page has the wording for the two situations that come up in almost every first session.

No pre-survey and none of the catalog decks are needed for this. They are in Section 2, for the session where a specific group needs something different.

The session, and the 90-minute extension

All three run on one deck, Getting started with Gemini in Sales (ttt-7-basics-session). One click copies it into your own Google Slides: see how the copy works, which also shows how to skip slides. Slide numbering counts the automatic title as slide 1.

Take the 90-minute version when the slot allows it and Gemini in Gmail is available. Two other shapes exist around it: the fifteen-minute first look inside an existing team meeting (deck ttt-8-team-meeting-intro), and the sales-cycle sprint for a group that asks for the whole cycle (deck ttt-9-course-sprint, half or full day, Section 3).

One check per exercise

Each exercise ends by comparing the result with the material it came from. In the pipeline exercise that is one segment added by hand from the account rows. In the report exercise it is one stage total, the largest open deal in the value column, and the date behind any claim about recent contact. In the email exercise it is the three supplied questions, the supplied facts, and the draft staying unsent. Allow time for that comparison, and for the questions it raises.

The run sheets carry the wording and the timing for each of these.

Nobody needs customer data

Every exercise runs on supplied fictional material: the practice tracker for the data cards, a practice inquiry for the email card, and NorthStar Facility Services for the sales-cycle course. Explain what the example contains and that participants can practise without supplying their own customer information. The data rules themselves are in Section 3.

2. Adapt for a Specific Group

Use this when the prepared plan does not fit: a leadership group that does not sell itself, a room that has already done the basics, or a market that asked for something specific. The starting point stays one of the three run sheets; you change what goes into the hands-on blocks.

Use the group information to support the same task

For a prepared format, keep its task, slide selection, time budget and checks. Use experience and role information to adjust the explanation, organise pairs or make the transfer question relevant. A leadership group can discuss how it would use the checked report in its team; sellers can name a question about their own account list.

If the group needs a different tool or sales-cycle task, plan that as a separate adaptation. Do not replace the email block with several course steps and assume the original slides and timing still fit. The longer course is described in Section 3.

An optional survey can reveal experience, priorities and concerns. Record how many people responded and whose perspective may be missing. Summarise wishes anonymously; do not invent quotes or treat a small response set as the view of the whole team.

What you have for this

Make your own copy of the pre-survey and send your copy’s link, so the responses land in your own Sheet. To see what a set of answers looks like before you send anything, open an example result sheet (seller group, leader group; synthetic answers, no real people).

For the plan itself, work from the run sheet for your length and the deck notes for the slides you show. Both are written out step by step, and the session scripts on the hub let you read a whole session as one page.

Two examples within the prepared format

A group of field sellers new to AI. Pair participants where access or confidence is uneven, and keep the five-minute source check and the closing task.

An area-sales leadership group. In the transfer step, ask which pipeline question they would repeat in their team and which figures they would verify. The task, slides and minutes stay as written.

3. Facilitation, Data Rules, and the Longer Formats

When the output is wrong, slow down

A table that does not match its source rows is a useful opportunity to practise the check. Add the segment together out loud, name the difference, and ask which of the two numbers they would send to their manager. Do not explain why the model erred, and do not take the keyboard. The Prepare page has the wording for this moment.

Demoing live

Confirm sign-ins before you start, not at the first prompt. Read a prompt aloud as you copy it, so people follow the thinking rather than the keystrokes. Use the deck’s screenshots and written examples to illustrate the workflow. Compare the result of your own demonstration with the rows in front of you. In the sales-cycle sprint, the Deep Research run on ISS (issworld.com, a real public facility-services company) is the slowest moment: start it live, walk the room through the NorthStar buying center while it works, then come back to it and verify one claim against its source in front of everyone.

The data rules

In approved Kärcher Workspace tools, signed in with the Kärcher account, teams may work with real customer data: the contract keeps it confidential and the tools do not learn from it. Two obligations stay with the user: share only what the task needs, and validate every claim before it reaches a customer. Formal proposals, pricing, legal and contract terms keep the normal sign-off. Say this in the first ten minutes of any session where real accounts might be opened. Nothing in the practice exercises requires it, because all of them run on fictional material.

Prepared answers to the recurring worries (data security, wrong answers, “will AI replace me”, CI, effort) are on the hub’s FAQ page.

The sales-cycle sprint (optional, half or full day)

For a group that asks for the whole cycle. The deck is ttt-9-course-sprint; the content is the nine course steps on the platform, which carry the exact prompt, the attachment and the example Doc on screen. Present from the platform, and name the hand-off out loud, because every step hands its output to the next: lead → sourced briefing and Go/No-Go → buying-center map → first email → need profile → value argument → demo storyboard → signal read → follow-up. Underneath it runs the value hypothesis: it forms in research (step 2), feeds the first email (step 3) and the discovery questions (step 5), is confirmed against the real need, and becomes the substantiated value argument (step 6). Rough budget: opening and data rules 20 minutes, then 15 to 30 minutes per step, with steps 2, 5 and 6 getting the depth. Cluster B groups replace steps 1 to 8 with the Your-data cards, step 9 and the leadership chapters, about 30 minutes.

The per-step goals, lead Gems and “don’t skip” points live with the steps themselves on the platform, which is where they stay current.

4. Checks and Support

Before the session

Where to get help

  • The content itself: the platform — the guide holds the depth on every card and course step.
  • Running a session: the three run sheets and the session scripts on the hub (Section 1).
  • Adapting one: Section 2 of this playbook; the pre-survey comes from Sales Enablement.
  • Sales Enablement / program owner: for accounts, the pre-survey form, and any client-specific compliance questions, contact your Kärcher Sales Enablement point of contact before your first session. Agree that named contact for access and content questions before a wider rollout.
  • After the session: the handoff, the platform link in the meeting notes, the next group or the longer format: see After the session on the hub.
