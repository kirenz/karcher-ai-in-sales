Companion document to the trainer hub. It does not teach the AI content (the learning platform carries that) and it does not repeat the session decks (they carry the full spoken script and the exercise click paths). It holds the four things that exist only here: the selection logic per group, the run sheet for a sprint day, two facilitation points specific to this program, and the pre-session checklist.

1. Select the Right Content per Group

One session cannot be optimal for a Key Account Manager and a Sales Director at the same time. The pre-survey sorts the room; the agenda follows from it.

The clusters and their core

Cluster A — Key Account & Field Sales. KAMs, direct reps, business development. Complex consultative selling, several stakeholders, individual offers. Core: the sales-cycle course on NorthStar, with full lesson depth (idea → recipe → example Doc) on research (step 2), needs analysis (step 5), and value argumentation (step 6).

Cluster B — Sales Leadership & Management. Directors, area and national sales managers, center managers. Steering pipeline, coaching, strategy, reporting. Core: the Your-data cards run on the practice tracker, step 9 (follow-up & steering), the leadership chapters in the guide, and the team routine they will install.

Mixed room. The course core plus whatever scores highest in the grid: a shared opening (tour, data-safety Basic, one Your-data card), then course steps for the sellers and step 9 plus the leadership chapters for the leaders. Assign the cluster by the work people describe, not by title; an Online Director lands in B.

The four reading rules

What you read in the results What it decides
The focus question (sell yourself, or steer a team) Cluster A or B, and therefore the core above
AI experience (Beginner / Basic / Intermediate / Advanced) How much foundations time. A Beginner/Basic majority means guided tour first, then the prompting and data-safety Basics
The usefulness grid (rows 1–6 are the Quick-Help groups, row 7 is the course) Which doors you open; the top rows name it literally. Reference pattern from the original survey across 75 colleagues: data & pipeline first, then meeting prep & research, then proposals & content
Expectations and concerns (free text) Your opening: quote two of the “one thing I want” answers; the data-safety Basic answers most of the concerns; someone who already uses AI can show it

Aim for responses from at least half the group, read them in the Forms summary view.

The three instruments

Make your own copy of the pre-survey and send your copy’s link, so the responses land in your own Sheet. The Session-Planner Gem drafts the plan from that Sheet; the Trainer Notebook answers questions about the materials with citations. To try the Gem before you have a group, copy an example result sheet (seller group, leader group; synthetic answers, no real people) and attach it.

Worked example: two groups, two agendas

Group A — twelve Key Account Managers, mostly Intermediate, top needs research and proposals. Multi-stakeholder consultative selling, so they get a course sprint:

Tour (2 min) → data-safety Basic → step 2 Research (heavy, with the live ISS demo) → step 3 First contact → step 5 Needs analysis → step 6 Value argument → step 7 Presentation & demo → step 9 Follow-up → commitments. ~4 hours. Accent: full lesson depth on steps 2, 5, and 6; the translation card as a side bite.

Group B — ten Area Sales Managers and Center Managers, top needs pipeline and reporting. They steer teams rather than run individual deals, so the agenda tilts to oversight and enablement:

Tour (2 min) → See your pipeline at a glance (everyone runs it on the practice tracker) → Turn your sheet into a short report → step 9 Follow-up & steering → the leadership chapters in the guide (pipeline steering, coaching) → how to run “card of the week” with their own teams → commitments. ~3 hours.

2. Run Sheet for the Sprint Day

Per unit: the goal, the lead Gem, the key point, and the one thing not to skip. The lesson on the platform is the content, with the exact prompt, the attachment, and the example Doc on screen. Keep the platform open and present from it.

The chain: every step hands its output to the next, all on NorthStar. Lead → sourced briefing and Go/No-Go → buying-center map → first email → need profile → value argument → decision moment → follow-up. Name the hand-off out loud (“the briefing you just made is what you attach in the next step”).

The value-hypothesis thread underneath it: the first hypothesis (why Kärcher might matter to this customer) forms in research (step 2), feeds the first email (step 3) and the discovery questions (step 5), is confirmed against the real need in discovery, and becomes the substantiated value argument (step 6). Pointing to it keeps each output specific to NorthStar instead of generic.

Opening block — the tour + the gate (~20 min)

  • Goal: the platform introduces itself; the data green light and the two-step gate are installed before any tool use.
  • How: click “Show me around” (the tour speaks for itself), then open the data-safety Basic together.
  • Key points: in approved Workspace tools, signed in, real customer data is allowed, share only what the task needs, then verify; the gate is two steps, Prompt with the right context, Validate the output; formal proposals, pricing, and contract terms keep the normal sign-off.
  • Don’t skip: the green light paired with the human-in-the-loop rule.

Step 1 — Acquisition (~20 min, Cluster A)

  • Goal: two roads to a lead, inbound (industry briefing → posts) and outbound (a sourced target shortlist).
  • Lead Gems: Industry-Research Gem, Post-Gem, Lead-Finder Gem.
  • Key point: the briefing is internal, never published; each post shares one insight and ends in a question.
  • Don’t skip: the hand-off, a reply to a post or a shortlist name is the lead that step 2 researches.

Step 2 — Research & Qualification (~30 min, the live moment)

  • Goal: a company name becomes a sourced briefing and a clear decision: pursue or pass.
  • Lead Gem: Account-Briefing Gem with Deep Research, run live on ISS (issworld.com), a real, public facility-services company.
  • The live demo, in order: launch the Gem on ISS yourself, pre-run result ready as fallback. While it runs, walk the room through the NorthStar buying center (Lars operations, Sophie procurement, Milan site, Eva HSE). When the result returns, verify one claim against its source in front of everyone. Then participants read the ready-made ISS briefing Doc from the lesson and make the Go/No-Go call themselves, naming the two or three to-verify claims.
  • Key point: facts separated from hypotheses; the rep makes the Go/No-Go, never the AI. The value-hypothesis thread starts here.
  • Don’t skip: flagging anything presented as fact that was a hypothesis.

Step 3 — First Contact (~20 min, Cluster A)

  • Goal: map the buying center from the briefing, then one role-fit first email that wins a meeting, not the deal.
  • Lead Gems: Buying-Center Gem, then the first-email recipe.
  • Key point: the four NorthStar personas and the role logic (decision-maker → impact, procurement → cost and risk, user → daily work); the email stays a Gmail draft until the rep sends it.
  • Don’t skip: the map returns in steps 4 and 5, say so.

Step 4 — Conversation Preparation (~10 min, Cluster A, light)

  • Goal: prepare the human side, opener, what to listen for, a loose style read.
  • Lead Gem: Rapport Gem (map + the person’s actual reply go in).
  • Key point: the style read is a hypothesis to hold lightly, never an AI profile from public data.
  • Don’t skip: the one line, the meeting itself stays human.

Step 5 — Needs Analysis (~25 min, the middle of the cycle)

  • Goal: sharp questions in, a clear need profile out.
  • Lead Gem: Needs-Analysis Gem, two recipes: the question set before the meeting, tagging the notes after.
  • Key point: questions get the customer talking, not pitching; the profile tags six dimensions with evidence, and a gap becomes a follow-up question, never a guess.
  • Don’t skip: the link that discovery must precede argumentation.

Step 6 — Value Argumentation (~25 min, core)

  • Goal: the confirmed need profile becomes a customer-specific value argument.
  • Lead Gem: Value-Argument Gem (need profile + BR 45/22 YellowFile attached).
  • Key point: feature → advantage → benefit → check, ordered by confirmed needs, in two role variants (Lars operational, Sophie commercial/total-cost); every product fact from the YellowFile, prices never in customer-facing output.
  • Don’t skip: every benefit must trace back to a discovered need, cut what does not map.

Step 7 — Presentation & Demonstration (~20 min, core)

  • Goal: a lean three-slide story (situation, fit, decision) and a demo storyboard that tell one story.
  • Lead Gems: the presentation recipe (Canvas → Slides draft, content only) and the Demo Gem.
  • Key point: the rep applies the Kärcher template and CI before anything goes out; every slide claim has a matching proof in the demo.
  • Don’t skip: the quality check before “sending”, substance, data, fit, next step.

Step 8 — Closing (~15 min, calm by design)

  • Goal: read the buying signal, choose the right path, answer objections calmly, capture the result.
  • Lead Gem: the closing coach (value block + short meeting notes in).
  • Key point: in the worked example the signal is weak and the coach recommends clarifying value first; the price objection gets a three-move answer (understand, reframe to cost-over-time, concrete next step).
  • Don’t skip: the summary keeps agreed and open points separate, with no invented commitments.

Step 9 — Follow-up & Steering (~20 min)

  • Goal: the meeting summary becomes a follow-up email, a four-line handover, one owned next step, and a light account segment.
  • Lead Gem: Follow-up & Handover Gem.
  • Key point: sent within twenty-four hours; where no date was agreed, the output says “to verify” instead of inventing one.
  • Don’t skip: one concrete next step with a named owner.

Leadership block (Cluster B, ~30 min)

  • Goal: leaders steer with the same tools: pipeline reading, coaching cues, team routine.
  • Material: the Your-data cards (pipeline at a glance, sheet to report) on the practice tracker; the leadership chapters in the guide; the Pipeline-Steering and Coaching & Steering Gems.
  • Key point: AI sorts the list, the leader decides which account deserves the team’s week.
  • Don’t skip: the routine to install, the link in the team meeting, the card of the week, one question in the meeting after.

3. Two Things Only This Program Needs

Demoing live without losing the room

The ISS Deep Research run is the most persuasive moment of the day and the slowest one. Start it live, talk through the NorthStar buying center while it works, then come back to the result. Read the recipe’s prompt aloud as you copy it, so people follow the thinking rather than the keystrokes. For the presentation step, work in Google Slides with Gemini in the side panel, not in a separate chat window.

  • Keep a pre-run result or a screenshot of every demo. The Train-the-Trainer catalog deck carries every screen and real output as a slide and is the ready-made fallback.
  • Confirm sign-ins before you start, not at the first prompt.

The gate as a habit, not a lecture

The quality gate is two steps, Prompt · Validate. Every time you prompt, narrate the green light out loud (“NorthStar is fictional, so I prompt freely; with a real account in an approved tool I would still share only the fields the task needs”). Every time AI returns numbers, ask the room whether they would send this to a customer as-is.

  • The two demo cases model both sides: NorthStar is fictional (prompt freely), ISS is real and public (use it for live research and verify against the source).
  • Every lesson recipe ends with its own check line (ground every number, no invented commitments, trace benefits to needs). Point at it instead of carrying the rules yourself.

Prepared answers to the recurring worries (data security, hallucinations, “will AI replace me”, CI, effort) are on the hub’s FAQ page.
