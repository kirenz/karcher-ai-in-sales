What do I say when …

Ready answers to the objections you will hear, and the moves when leadership sees no time.

Worried about the hard questions? Thirty seconds.

← Trainer Hub

The objections below are the real ones, collected from the sales survey and the first trainings, roughly in the order you will hear them. Every answer stands on the same two feet: the approved data rules apply, and a person checks the output before it is used.

“Is this safe? What about data security and compliance?”
In our approved tools, signed in with the Kärcher account, working with customer data is permitted: the Workspace agreement keeps it confidential and the content is not used to train the models. That is the company rule, and it does not remove your own responsibility. Share only the information the task actually needs, and check the output before it is used. To rank a pipeline, AI needs stage, value, and dates, not a customer's phone number. In the exercises we work on the practice tracker and on NorthStar, which are fictional, so nobody has to weigh that question while learning.
“Won't AI just replace me?”
AI does the sorting, drafting, and structuring. It does not decide which account deserves your week, build the relationship in the room, or own the customer promise. AI sorts the list; you decide which account deserves your week. The sellers who win are the ones who let AI take the busywork so they spend more time with customers, not less.
“How can I trust the numbers? It makes things up.”
You are right not to trust it blindly. AI can state something wrong with full confidence, and answers built on your own documents can still be wrong or incomplete, so we build two habits instead of promising accuracy: tell AI to flag missing data rather than invent it, and check the result against the source before anything is used. The platform's example documents model this: where no date was agreed, the follow-up says “to verify” instead of inventing one. Every exercise ends with a check for that reason.
“I'll lose my own voice and style.”
AI gives you a fast first draft, not a final send. Your voice comes from the editing pass, the part you keep. The first-contact lesson is explicit: the email stays a draft until you have read it, edited it, and send it in your own words. A draft you reshape is still your voice; a blank page late at night is the real threat to it.
“The output isn't in Kärcher corporate identity.”
Correct: raw output is a draft, not customer-ready material. AI gets you to a strong draft fast; you then apply the Kärcher template, the approved claims, and the CI before anything goes out. For customer-facing material the normal sign-off still applies on top.
“Is the effort worth it? I'm already busy.”
That busy week that moved nothing is exactly the problem this solves. The first wins are small and immediate: your pipeline value in one prompt, a deck outline in minutes instead of an evening. You don't adopt everything; you adopt one card that gives time back this week.
“We already had an AI training from IT.”
Good, this is the other half. IT teaches how the tools work; this program teaches where they fit in a sales week. The platform's cards and course are organized by sales tasks and the sales cycle, not by tools. You pick a sales task, and the tool comes with it.
“It's in English. My team won't use it.”
The written material is English, and your browser translates it: one right-click turns the platform, this hub and every session script into their language, and the globe icon on each page opens a short guide that shows how. The AI itself speaks their language too: Gemini and the prepared Gems answer in whatever language they are written to, so coaching, negotiation practice, and customer emails all happen locally. The slide files stay English; you present them in the language of the room. If your market needs a translated edition, tell us, so the demand is on record.
“We don't even have a CRM here.”
The whole program is CRM-free by design; Google Sheets stands in for the pipeline. Markets and channels without a CRM are fully included, and saying that out loud early lowers the barrier for half the room.
“My leadership sees no time for this.”
Then ask for a smaller slot: thirty minutes, or fifteen minutes inside a meeting that already exists, and bring something from their own team with you. The full set of moves is below: when leadership pushes back.

When leadership pushes back

The most common blocker is not a skeptical seller; it is a sales leader who sees no time. Take it seriously: their calendar is full of revenue, and you are asking for a slice of it. These moves work because they make the slice small and the return visible.

  • Make the ask small. Never open with a half-day. Open with fifteen minutes inside a meeting that already exists, the first-look deck: no new slot to find. The longer session is the second ask, once the room has seen a card run live.
  • Argue with their own numbers. If you ran the optional pre-survey, show their team's answers: where the week goes, where people ask for help. If not, bring what people produced in your last session in that market. Either way it stops being "an initiative from the Academy" and becomes "what your people did".
  • Lead with the card the leader feels. The pipeline card produces the artifact a sales leader wants anyway: pipeline value by stage and segment, from the team's own sheet, in minutes.
  • Answer the risk question before it is asked.
Say"In our approved Workspace tools, signed in with the Kärcher account, working with customer data is allowed; the tools never train on it. And nothing reaches a customer unchecked, the check is part of every lesson."

If the answer is still no, ask for the smallest thing: the platform link in the team's meeting notes, and two minutes to mention it. Find the one seller who already uses AI and let their win travel; leaders move for peer results faster than for programs. And tell us, so the program knows where trainers need backup. You are not expected to win every room alone.
