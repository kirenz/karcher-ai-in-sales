FAQ · Objection handling

What do I say when …

Ready answers to the ten objections you will actually hear.

Worried about the hard questions? Thirty seconds.

← Trainer hub

The objections below are the real ones, collected from the sales survey and the first trainings, roughly in the order you will hear them. Every answer stands on the same two feet: the data rules are built in, and a person checks before anything reaches a customer.

“Is this safe? What about data security and compliance?”
Good news first: in our approved tools, signed in with the Kärcher account, you are allowed to work with real customer data; the Workspace contract keeps it confidential and the tools never train on it. Your part is light but real: share only the information the task actually needs, then verify the output. To rank a pipeline, AI needs stage, value, and dates, not a customer's phone number. In the sessions we practice on NorthStar, which is fictional, so everyone can prompt freely.
“Won't AI just replace me?”
AI does the sorting, drafting, and structuring. It does not decide which account deserves your week, build the relationship in the room, or own the customer promise. AI sorts the list; you decide which account deserves your week. The sellers who win are the ones who let AI take the busywork so they spend more time with customers, not less.
“How can I trust the numbers? It makes things up.”
You are right not to trust it blindly; that is the job, not a flaw in the training. So we build two reflexes: tell AI to flag missing data rather than invent it, and check every result before anything customer-facing. The platform's own example documents model this: where no date was agreed, the follow-up says “to verify” instead of inventing one. AI drafts; you verify.
“I'll lose my own voice and style.”
AI gives you a fast first draft, not a final send. Your voice comes from the editing pass, the part you keep. The first-contact lesson is explicit: the email stays a draft until you have read it, edited it, and send it in your own words. A draft you reshape is still your voice; a blank page late at night is the real threat to it.
“The output isn't in Kärcher corporate identity.”
Correct, and raw output is never customer-ready. AI gets you to a strong draft fast; you then apply the Kärcher template, the approved claims, and the CI before anything goes out. For customer-facing material the normal sign-off still applies on top.
“Is the effort worth it? I'm already busy.”
That busy week that moved nothing is exactly the problem this solves. The first wins are small and immediate: your pipeline value in one prompt, a deck outline in minutes instead of an evening. You don't adopt everything; you adopt one card that gives time back this week.
“We already had an AI training from IT.”
Good, this is the other half. IT teaches how the tools work; this program teaches where they fit in a sales week. The platform's cards and course are organized by sales tasks and the sales cycle, not by tools. You pick a sales task, and the tool comes with it.
“It's in English. My team won't use it.”
Nobody has to wait for a translation: Chrome translates the whole platform in one click, and the globe icon on every page opens a short guide that shows how, greeting your team in Kärcher's twelve languages. More importantly, the AI itself speaks their language: Gemini and the prepared Gems answer in whatever language they are written to, so coaching, negotiation practice, and customer emails all happen locally. Only these pages and the spoken audio stay English. Full language editions can follow demand, market by market; if your market needs one, say so in your Academy note.
“We don't even have a CRM here.”
The whole program is CRM-free by design; Google Sheets stands in for the pipeline. Markets and channels without a CRM are fully included, and saying that out loud early lowers the barrier for half the room.
“My leadership sees no time for this.”
Then don't ask for time; ask for fifteen minutes inside a meeting that already exists, and bring their own team's survey answers with you. The full set of moves has its own page: When leadership pushes back.
