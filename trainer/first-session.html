1 · Try the exercise yourself, about 15 minutes

You will use Gemini in Google Sheets to turn a customer list into an overview of its sales values. Then you will check one total against the original list. This is the same exercise your participants will try.

What is the practice tracker?

The practice tracker is a prepared Google Sheets spreadsheet containing twelve fictional customer accounts. Each row describes one example account: its industry, its stage in the sales process, a value in euros, and notes such as the next step. These details are made up for the exercise. You do not need to collect or upload real customer information.

You will work on your own copy, so you can try things without changing the shared example.

Follow these steps

  1. Open the exercise instructions: See your pipeline at a glance. We call this an exercise card: it is a page with the instructions, a link to the example spreadsheet and ready-written text to copy into Gemini. That text is called a prompt; it tells the AI what you want it to do.
  2. Make your own copy of the spreadsheet. Open the practice tracker, then choose File → Make a copy. Open your copy and check that you can edit it. Keep the exercise card open in another browser tab so you can follow its steps.
  3. Look at the example data. Find the columns for sales stage, industry segment and value. A stage says how far a deal has progressed; a segment groups similar customers, such as Food & Beverage. Scroll through the twelve rows once so you know what the overview will summarise.
  4. Ask Gemini to create the overview. Follow the card to open Ask Gemini in your spreadsheet, then copy the prompt from the card and paste it unchanged. Gemini may first show a short plan with an Approve button; read it, then approve it. The intended result is a summary table of values by stage and segment on a new sheet tab called pipeline value. Its layout may look different from the slide example.
  5. Check one total, as the card asks. Pick a small segment in the original customer list, add its values yourself and compare the sum with Gemini's summary for that segment. If the numbers differ, look at which rows were included and correct the summary before using it. This is the check your participants will make too.

If the answer appears only in the side panel, ask again for the summary on a new tab with that name. If a button is missing or you cannot copy the spreadsheet, note where you got stuck and ask for help with access before your session.

2 · Pick your length and copy the deck, about 20 minutes

A run sheet is your step-by-step plan for the session: which slides to show, what participants do, how much time to allow, how to check the result, and what to shorten if you run late. Start with 30 minutes if your group is new to AI; the longer plans add practice to the same first exercise.

3 · Read it through once, about 10 minutes

Read your run sheet minute by minute, then the script page for the slides you show. Note the one moment you want to say slowly. Two situations come up in almost every first session; the wording below works.

The answer lands in the chat panel, not on a new tab.

Say"Ask for the table specifically: put the result on a new tab called pipeline value. Then we compare one total with the source rows." If it stays in the panel: "Fine, we work with what you have. Take the numbers from the panel, and we do the check on those. The check is the part I want you to practise."

Someone says a total looks wrong.

Say"Good, show me. Which segment is it? Read me the values in those rows and we add them together here, out loud." Then: "Our rows add up to this. If the summary shows the same figure, that segment matches. If it differs, we look at which rows it included and correct it."

You do not need to explain why the AI produced a difference, and you do not take the keyboard. Name the next action; the check settles it.

Before participants arrive

  • Every participant can sign in to Google Workspace with the Kärcher account and use Ask Gemini in Sheets (and in Gmail for the 90-minute session). If someone needs to share a device, plan time for both people to try the steps.
  • The deck is prepared: the slides outside your selection are skipped.
  • Links ready to paste in the chat: the exercise card, the practice tracker, and for 90 minutes the email card.
  • Smoke test in the room: the platform loads, one card opens, the practice tracker copies. Projector, side-panel visibility, sound.
  • You have read If time runs short in your run sheet, so you know which activity to shorten while leaving time for the check and the closing discussion.

Adapt a session, only when the prepared one does not fit

Use these when a group has very different experience levels, a market has its own priorities, or people already ran a session. Change how you explain the task, how you pair people and how you discuss applying it at work; keep the task, the check and the minutes as the run sheet has them.
