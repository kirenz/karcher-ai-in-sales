Teaching AI to a mixed-experience sales room

Most of your room is Basic or Intermediate. Three principles keep them with you:

  • Lead with the pain, not the technology. Open each unit with the work problem (“a busy week that moved nothing”), then show AI as the relief. Never open with “let me explain how large language models work.”
  • One concrete win per unit. Aim for each person to leave each block with something they could use Monday: a ranked list, a briefing, a three-slide outline. Concrete beats comprehensive.
  • Show, then hand over. Demonstrate the move on NorthStar, then immediately have them repeat it — the lessons are built for exactly this, every recipe has the prompt to copy and the example Doc to compare with. The “now do it with your own account” handoff (Section 6) is where learning sticks.

Keep frameworks in the background. The need dimensions and value chains are useful logic underneath the lessons, but they are tools, not subjects. Say “better discovery questions,” not an acronym. And let the platform do its share of the talking: the tour introduces the structure, the Listen buttons read lessons aloud, the quizzes lock ideas in — you facilitate, you do not lecture.

Demoing tools live without losing the room

Live demos are powerful and risky. Protect yourself:

  • Have a fallback. Keep a screenshot or a pre-run result of each demo (especially the ISS Deep Research run, which can take minutes). The Train-the-Trainer catalog deck carries every screen and real output as a slide — it is your ready-made fallback. If the live run stalls, show the slide and narrate.
  • Narrate the prompt, not the typing. Read the recipe’s prompt aloud as you copy it so people learn the thinking, not the keystrokes.
  • Pre-run the slow ones. Deep Research takes time. Start the live ISS run, then talk through the buying center while it works, and return to the result.
  • Use the side panel where it lives. For presentations, work in Google Slides with Gemini in the side panel so people see the real workflow, not a separate chat window.
  • Keep accounts ready. Confirm sign-ins before you start. Nothing kills momentum like ten people stuck at a login screen.

Making the gate a habit, not a lecture

  • The quality gate (Prompt · Validate): do not deliver it as a compliance lecture. Make it a reflex you model. Every time you prompt, narrate the green light out loud (“NorthStar is fictional, so I prompt freely; with a real account in an approved tool I would still share only the fields the task needs”). Every time AI returns numbers, pause and ask the room “would you send this to a customer as-is?” That two-second pause, repeated, is the lesson.
  • The green light, always visible: in approved tools, signed in, real customer data is allowed; the rep’s job is to share the fields the task needs and verify the output. In sessions we model this on NorthStar (fictional, so prompt freely) and ISS (real and public, so use it for live research and verify against the source).
  • The check lives in the recipes. Every lesson recipe ends with its own check line (ground every number, no invented commitments, trace benefits to needs). Point at it; you never have to carry the rules alone.

Managing energy, timing, and group dynamics

  • Budget, don’t script. The unit times are a budget. If step 2 runs long because the ISS demo lands well, take it from a lighter unit, not from the opening gate.
  • Adapt depth to the room:
    • Skeptical group — go slower on the gate and verification, lean on the worked example and the real result Docs, invite their worries early (Section 7), and let one good result speak for itself. Do not oversell.
    • Enthusiastic group — move faster through demos, spend the saved time on “now do it with your own account,” and stretch into more cards.
  • Protect the hands-on time. When you are behind, cut your own talking, not the participants’ practice. The doing is the value.
  • Read the energy after lunch. A natural dip: reset it with a Quick-Help card everyone runs — a result in five minutes beats any energizer.
