When leadership pushes back

Small asks, their own numbers, and one artifact that argues for you.

Leadership says no time? Thirty seconds.

← Trainer hub

The most common blocker is not a skeptical seller; it is a sales leader who sees no time. Take it seriously: their calendar is full of revenue, and you are asking for a slice of it. These moves work because they make the slice small and the return visible.

Make the ask small

Never open with a half-day. Open with fifteen minutes inside a meeting that already exists, the first-look deck. There is no calendar cost, so there is nothing to refuse. The sixty-minute session is the second ask, and by then the room has seen a card run live.

Argue with their own numbers

You have the pre-survey. Show the leader their own team's answers: where the week goes, where people ask for help, how they rate their own skill. This stops being "an initiative from the Academy" and becomes "what your team said last week".

Say"I asked your team. Their top time eaters are proposals, pipeline data, and research; none of that is selling. I can give them one hour on exactly those tasks, on our own platform."

Lead with the card the leader feels

Start with the pipeline card. It produces the artifact a sales leader wants anyway: pipeline value by stage and segment, from the team's own sheet, in minutes. When the output lands on their desk, the next session sells itself.

Answer the risk question before it is asked

Say"In our approved Workspace tools, signed in with the Kärcher account, working with customer data is allowed; the tools never train on it. And nothing reaches a customer unchecked, the check is part of every lesson."

If the answer is still no

  • Ask for the smallest thing: the platform link in the team's meeting notes, and two minutes to mention it.
  • Find the one seller who already uses AI (your survey shows them) and let their win travel; leaders move for peer results faster than for programs.
  • Note it in your Academy note as a blocker. That is what the channel is for; you are not expected to win every room alone.
