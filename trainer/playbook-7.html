These are the real worries from the survey (n=75), in roughly the order they came up. The two biggest are data security/compliance and trust in AI numbers (hallucinations). Closing/objection skill was the lowest need, so do not over-prepare for sales-technique pushback — prepare for these adoption worries instead. Every answer anchors in the gate and the human-in-the-loop rule.

“Is this safe? What about data security and compliance?”

This is the number-one worry, so meet it head-on, early, with the gate — not as a disclaimer at the end.

Reframe: “Good news first: in our approved tools, signed in with your Kärcher account, you are allowed to work with real customer data — the Workspace contract keeps it confidential and the tools never train on it. Your part is light but real: share only the information the task actually needs, then verify the output. To rank a pipeline, AI needs stage, value, and dates, not a customer’s phone number. Less data is both safer and sharper. In the sessions we practise on NorthStar, which is fictional, so you can prompt freely, and on ISS, which is real and public, so you use it for live research and verify it.”

Point to the Prompt · Validate gate from the data-safety Basic, and note that the normal sign-off still applies to formal proposals, pricing, and contract terms.

“Won’t AI just replace me?”

Reframe: “AI does the sorting, drafting, and structuring. It does not decide which account deserves your week, build the relationship in the room, or own the customer promise. The line is exact: AI sorts the list, you decide which account deserves your week. The reps who win are the ones who let AI take the busywork so they spend more time with customers, not less.”

“How can I trust the numbers? It makes things up.”

This is the second-biggest worry, and a legitimate one.

Reframe: “You are right not to trust it blindly — that is the job, not a flaw in the training. Confident output is not verified output. So we build two reflexes: tell AI to flag missing data rather than invent it, and run the Validate step before anything customer-facing. The platform’s own outputs model this: in the real NorthStar follow-up, where no date was agreed, the document says ‘to verify’ instead of inventing one. AI drafts; you verify; and the normal sign-off still applies to anything formal that goes out.”

“I’ll lose my own voice and style.”

Reframe: “AI gives you a fast first draft, not a final send. Your voice comes from the editing pass — the part you keep. The first-contact lesson is explicit: the email stays a Gmail draft until you have read it, edited it, and send it in your own words. A draft you reshape is still your voice; a blank page at 11pm is the real threat to it.”

“The output isn’t in Kärcher corporate identity / our brand.”

Reframe: “Correct, and we don’t expect raw output to be customer-ready. AI gets you to a strong draft fast; you then apply the Kärcher template, the approved claims, and the CI before anything goes out. The presentation lesson builds this in: the draft exports as content only, the final deck is built in our templates — and for customer-facing decks the normal sign-off still applies on top.”

“Is the effort worth the benefit? I’m already busy.”

Reframe: “That busy week that moved nothing is exactly the problem this solves. The first wins are small and immediate: your pipeline value in one prompt, a customer deck outline in minutes instead of an evening. Start with one card — the card of the week is exactly that, one use case, one habit. You don’t adopt everything; you adopt one thing that gives time back this week.”

“We already had an AI training from IT.”

Reframe: “Good — this is the other half. IT teaches how the tools work. This program teaches where they fit in a sales week: the platform’s cards and course are organized by sales tasks and the sales cycle, not by tools. Nobody studies a tool for its own sake here; you pick a sales task, and the tool comes with it.”

Region and channel adaptation notes

  • Translation/localization: for international field teams, lean into the translation card and the localization angle in first contact and presentations; always have a native speaker sanity-check anything customer-facing.
  • Language of the platform: it is in English today, but nobody has to wait: Chrome translates the whole platform in one click, and the platform’s own language guide (the globe icon on every page) shows how, opening with a recognition line in Kärcher’s 12 languages. Only the spoken audio stays English. Beyond that, the platform is built for full language versions — a language edition is a complete platform with every text and audio track in that language. If your market needs one, say so; languages follow demand.
  • No-CRM channels: the whole program is CRM-free by design (Google Sheets stands in), so colleagues without a CRM are fully included — say so explicitly; it lowers the barrier.
  • Local examples: keep NorthStar as the running example for continuity. Swap in a local account only when it genuinely helps, and never invent data for it — use ISS-style research on a real, public company instead.
  • Leadership rooms (Cluster B): expect more questions about team rollout and reporting than about individual deals. Steer toward the data cards, the leadership chapters, and the routine moves.
