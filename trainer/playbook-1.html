Who this is for

You are an international Kärcher trainer who will deliver the AI in Sales program to sales colleagues in your country. You do not need to be an AI expert. You need to be a confident facilitator who can explain a clear structure, run a few hands-on moments, and handle questions honestly. This playbook assumes you have attended the Train-the-Trainer workshop, or will.

The participants you will train are mostly Basic or Intermediate with AI (91% in our survey). That is good news: you are not competing with experts. You are showing busy salespeople a few concrete moves that save them time this week.

What you deliver from

Everything lives on the learning platform: karcher-ai-sales.web.app, sign-in with the Kärcher Google account. You do not rebuild content; you open it.

The platform has five doors:

  • Quick help — one task card per everyday job, sorted in groups from emails to coaching, each one a copied prompt and about five minutes, with ready-made practice material on the card. The newest group, Sales Coaching & Practice, holds practice Gems from the trainer community.
  • AI Basics — short foundations (what AI is for, prompting, Gemini & Gems, Deep Research, NotebookLM, Workspace Studio, data safety).
  • The sales-cycle course — the nine steps from acquisition to follow-up, worked end to end on one example customer (NorthStar), each step with an idea page, a quick check, and recipes: the exact prompt, what to attach, the Gem one click away, and a ready-made example Doc to compare with.
  • The guide — the full handbook behind everything, including the sales-leadership track.
  • The notebook — the same handbook as a knowledge base: ask in your own words, get answers with citations.

Every lesson can also be listened to, and a guided tour introduces the platform by itself — click “Start the tour” on the start page.

On top of the platform you have six things of your own:

  • The pre-survey (Google Forms) — your selection instrument: make your own copy of the form, then send your copy’s link, so the answers land in your own response Sheet. Section 3 explains how to read them.
  • The Train-the-Trainer catalog deck — the slide catalog you received at the workshop: every door, card, lesson screen, and example output as a slide. Use it as your flip-book and projector fallback; in the room, prefer showing the platform itself.
  • The session decks — ready-to-present decks for participants, one per session format, each with the complete spoken script in the notes and “Your turn” exercise slides that carry the click paths: the Basics session (the tools, prompting, the data gate), the team-meeting intro (fifteen minutes, everyone runs one card), and the course sprint (the nine steps, every document built by the group). Present them as they are, hide what your session does not need, or translate them for your market.
  • The Session-Planner Gem — attach the response Sheet of your pre-survey copy, tell it the session size, and it drafts your plan with the program’s reading rules: blocks, material, opening quotes, what to skip. Open the Session-Planner Gem. To try it before you have a group, copy one of the two example result sheets on the hub’s planning page (seller group, leader group; synthetic answers, no real people) and attach the copy.
  • The Trainer Notebook (NotebookLM) — this playbook, the catalog deck, and the pre-survey as a knowledge base. Ask it anything about the materials, which cards fit a leader group, what to say to an objection, which lesson explains the data gate; it answers only from these materials, with citations. Planning a session from survey results is the Session-Planner Gem’s job. Open the Trainer Notebook (public link, no sign-in needed).
  • This playbook.

The one habit that carries everything

Select per group, then deliver. Do not walk every group through everything. Send the pre-survey first, read which cluster the group falls into and what they need most, pick the matching doors and lessons, then deliver with NorthStar as the one running example. Everything else in this playbook supports that single habit.
