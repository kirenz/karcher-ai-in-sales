Rehearsal · 10 min

Try the session on one colleague before the real room.

Prepare30 minutes60 minutes90 minutesRehearsal · 10 min

Ten minutes with one colleague before your first session. Together you try explaining the exercise, helping someone who is stuck, and comparing a total with the rows it came from.

The practice tracker is a prepared Google Sheets spreadsheet with twelve fictional customer accounts and example sales values. You and your colleague use your own copies. If you have not tried it yet, follow the preparation guide first; it explains the spreadsheet and the exercise step by step.

What you need

  • Two people and one screen each. A meeting call works as well as a room.
  • Both of you: your own copy of the practice tracker (File · Make a copy), made before the ten minutes start.
  • The card open: See your pipeline at a glance.
  • Slide 10 of the session deck on screen, the way it will be during the exercise.

The two roles

Trainer. You explain and facilitate exactly as you will in the session. Let your colleague try the steps on their own screen. If they need help, explain the next step and give them time to try it.

Learner and observer. Your colleague does the exercise as a participant would, plays the two situations below, and keeps the three questions in mind. They give the feedback at the end.

The ten minutes

From Min What happens
0:00 1 Trainer explains the exercise; learner can ask for clarification
0:01 3 Learner works: opens Ask Gemini, runs the prompt from the card
0:04 2 Situation A — the learner gets stuck
0:06 2 Situation B — a total is questioned
0:08 1 Learner verifies one segment total by hand
0:09 1 Talk through the three questions, one sentence each

The three questions

Talk these through together in the final minute. Note one thing you would like to say differently.

  1. Was the task clear? Which instructions helped them start, and where did they need more explanation?
  2. Could the learner keep going? When something went wrong, which next step helped them continue?
  3. Did the check happen? Could you both follow the calculation and compare it with the original rows?

Situation A · the learner gets stuck

Your colleague plays this: they paste the prompt, and the answer appears in the chat panel on the right instead of on a new tab. They say: “It just wrote me a text. There is no new tab.”

Wording that works:

“Ask for the table specifically: put the result on a new tab called pipeline value. Then we compare one total with the source rows.”

If the second run also stays in the chat:

“Fine, we work with what you have. Take the numbers from the panel, and we do the check on those. The check is the part I actually want you to practise.”

What you are practising: naming one concrete next action, and not letting a tool problem become the learner’s problem.

Situation B · a total is questioned

Your colleague plays this: they have their table from the exercise, and they say one segment total looks too low. They are playing a doubt on purpose; the table may well be correct. That is the point: the check is what settles it.

Wording that works:

“Good, show me. Which segment is it? Read me the values in those rows and we add them together here, out loud.”

Then, once your own sum is on the table:

“Our rows add up to this. If the summary shows the same figure, that segment matches. If it differs, we look at which rows it included and correct it with our calculation.”

What you are practising: checking a result against the original rows and saying out loud whether it matched or differed. You do not need to diagnose how the AI produced a difference.

If no colleague is available

Practise the sequence aloud on your own copy of the tracker: explain the exercise, run the prompt, then check one segment total by hand and say the result out loud. That is a good preparation on its own. The one thing it cannot tell you is how your explanation lands with someone else, so ask a colleague to sit in on the first ten minutes of a session when you get the chance.

If you want to run a part again

If a step felt unclear, talk about what would help and try that part once more. Two or three minutes each:

Part to practise What to repeat
The task was not clear The explanation only, at 0:00. Say it in three sentences: what to open, what to paste, what “done” looks like.
The learner could not keep going Situation A only, with the wording above ready.
The check did not happen The last two minutes, and ask for it by name: “which segment did you check, and did it match?”

If a part still feels unfamiliar, it is a good candidate for the first thing you say slowly in the room. Co-facilitating a small 30-minute session with a colleague is another comfortable way in.
